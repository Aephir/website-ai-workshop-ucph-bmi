# Idea stress test

Before committing time to a plan, ask the AI to attack it instead of improve it. Improving a half-formed idea tends to polish the surface; attacking it surfaces the assumptions and failure modes you'd otherwise only find after you'd already started.

## The pattern

1. State the idea or plan as it currently stands.
2. Ask directly for the strongest case against it — not devil's advocate theater, the real weak points.
3. Ask which of those would actually kill the idea versus which are minor and fixable.

## Example prompt

> I'm planning to run a recurring lab meeting where each week one person presents a paper they didn't write, cold, with no prep beyond reading it once. Give me the strongest case against this working, not a balanced pro/con list. Then tell me which objections would actually kill the format and which are just friction that goes away after a few weeks.

## Why this works

A direct "is this a good idea?" tends to get validated with minor caveats — the model mirrors your framing. Asking specifically for the strongest case against it forces a real answer, and separating "kills it" from "just friction" stops you from either overreacting to a minor flaw or ignoring a real one.
